# Massless-spin-2 assumption-compression audit

**Decision:** conditional leading-order redundancy identified; **new active seam: NONE**; **calculation started: NO**. This compares established derivations against the [joint SM-plus-gravity EFT reference ledger](JOINT_SM_GR_EFT_REFERENCE_AUDIT.md). It does not select a microscopic graviton theory, derive all of GR from no premises, or change the quasiclassical-recovery requirement.

## Exact claim under audit

Can the reference ledger's **universal leading matter coupling** and **Einstein-Hilbert leading dynamics** be treated as consequences, rather than separately chosen postulates, *conditional on* a specified massless helicity-2 framework? Three routes answer different parts of this question. An implication inside a restricted framework is not yet a net reduction in the independent assumptions of a Theory of Everything.

## Three derivation routes

| Route | Premises doing the work | Earned conclusion | Authority ceiling / failure of overextension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soft graviton / S-matrix** — [Weinberg 1964](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049); compare [Weinberg 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.138.B988) | A Lorentz-invariant S-matrix with the specified one-particle pole structure; an interacting massless helicity-2 state; asymptotically defined scattering states and momentum conservation. The 1964 theorem states Lorentz invariance, pole structure, zero mass, and spin as its assumptions; do not silently add a separate unitarity theorem to that specific proof. | The leading soft-graviton pole cannot carry arbitrary species-dependent gravitational charges among particles participating in connected processes: the coupling is universal at that order. The 1965 perturbative work adds assumptions to connect physical helicities and long-range interactions to Einstein-type equations. | A leading soft Ward/factorization statement is **not** a derivation of a full curved-spacetime SM action, all finite-momentum vertices, $\xi$, higher-curvature terms, or classical spacetime. The flat/asymptotic S-matrix premise does not transfer automatically to cosmological or other non-asymptotically-flat backgrounds. Extra fields can add forces without changing this leading spin-2 statement. |
| **Spin-2 self-coupling/bootstrap** — [Deser 1970](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411023), clarified in [Deser 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2975) | A free linear massless spin-2 gauge field about Minkowski space; a local stress-tensor coupling to matter and gravitational energy; consistency of the gauge/constraint structure; the chosen two-derivative, first-order (Palatini) completion. A symmetric stress tensor and its improvement prescription are inputs, not free discoveries. | Self-coupling of the gravitational stress tensor closes into the Einstein action in Deser's variables; consistent coupling of the included matter can be organized through one effective metric. This supplies a constructive leading nonlinear completion. | The construction depends on the chosen stress-tensor/covariantization and derivative order. It does not prove uniqueness against all higher-derivative, nonlocal, extra-field, or alternative-background theories. Starting at Minkowski does not predict $\rho_\Lambda$; a nonzero cosmological term generally changes the background problem. Curvature-matter improvements remain possible. |
| **Geometric uniqueness** — [Lovelock 1971](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1665613) | A four-dimensional metric geometry and a symmetric, divergence-free rank-two local metric tensor built from the metric and up to its second derivatives, under the theorem's naturality/regularity conditions. The metric, dimension, and second-order restriction have already been assumed. | Within that class, the metric and Einstein tensors span the field-equation side: schematically $aG_{\mu\nu}+b g_{\mu\nu}$. Thus Einstein dynamics is the unique nontrivial two-derivative *metric* bulk equation in the specified class, up to normalization and cosmological term. | This does **not** derive a metric from helicity-2 particles, fix the source/matter action, remove $b$ or the coupling strength, or exclude higher-derivative EFT corrections, scalar-tensor/multifield extensions, or nonlocal terms. Four-dimensional Gauss-Bonnet is topological for constant coefficient in the local bulk setting, not a ban on all curvature-squared physics. |

For the first route, the relevant leading-pole logic can be summarized without claiming a new calculation. If species $i$ has soft coupling $\kappa_i$, emission from hard external legs has a pole proportional to

$$
\mathcal M_{n+1}^{\mu\nu}(q)\sim
\sum_i\eta_i\kappa_i\frac{p_i^\mu p_i^\nu}{p_i\cdot q}\,\mathcal M_n.
$$

Decoupling a pure-gauge polarization requires $\sum_i\eta_i\kappa_i p_i^\nu=0$. Ordinary momentum conservation supplies $\sum_i\eta_i p_i^\nu=0$; across generic connected processes this forces a common leading $\kappa_i$, not its numerical value. This is a conditional S-matrix result, not evidence that nature must contain a fundamental graviton. [Weinberg 1964](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049).

## Compression ledger against the reference EFT

| Reference-ledger entry | What the three routes actually reduce | What remains independent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Universal coupling of included matter to gravity | **Conditionally constrained at the leading soft pole** by the S-matrix route; a common-metric minimal completion is **constructible** in the bootstrap route. This is the strongest earned overlap. | Existence of the interacting single massless helicity-2 sector, pole/factorization and Lorentz assumptions, choice of connected matter sector, off-shell completion, stress-tensor improvement, and permitted nonminimal/higher-order matter-curvature terms. A common leading soft coupling is narrower than a uniquely fixed full metric/vierbein action. |
| Einstein-Hilbert leading dynamics | **Conditionally recovered** through the local two-derivative bootstrap, and **conditionally unique** after Lovelock's four-dimensional metric/second-order premises are supplied. | The field content, locality/derivative truncation, background/geometry premises, and the measured normalization $M_0^2$ remain. These routes do not establish a UV-complete quantum theory or a preferred cosmological state. |
| Cosmological term $\rho_\Lambda$ | Lovelock permits the metric-tensor term; a flat-space spin-2 expansion commonly works about a zero-effective-$\Lambda$ background. | Its physical value and vacuum-energy mechanism are not predicted; assuming a flat background must not be misreported as deriving $\Lambda=0$. |
| Higgs-curvature $\xi RH^\dagger H$ | **Not fixed.** The stress tensor can have improvements that leave total Poincaré charges unchanged while changing local gravitational couplings; curved-space SM renormalization also requires/runs $\xi$. | Its boundary value, scheme/scale, and operational combinations remain independent. A bootstrap that chooses minimal covariantization has made a choice, not eliminated this freedom. [Callan, Coleman & Jackiw 1970](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(70)90394-5); [Markkanen *et al.* 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02020). |
| $c_R,c_{\rm Ric}$, other local and nonlocal EFT terms | **Not fixed by leading soft universality or two-derivative uniqueness.** Their status is exactly the [reference audit's](JOINT_SM_GR_EFT_REFERENCE_AUDIT.md) local/nonlocal and field-redefinition ledger. | Local Wilson data, calculable loop terms conditional on light content, basis-invariant observables, boundaries/topology, and higher-order corrections all retain their separate qualifications. |
| SM gauge/flavor/Higgs inputs; quantum state and quasiclassical recovery | **Not fixed** by any of these routes. | Matter content and parameters, quantum-to-classical emergence, and tested observables remain distinct recovery obligations. |

**Assumption exchange, not a parameter count.** The comparison exposes a genuine *conditional relation*: within a single-massless-spin-2, Lorentz-invariant, suitable scattering/local-two-derivative setting, universal leading coupling and Einstein-type dynamics need not be separately tuned. It does **not** show that these premises are fewer, empirically cheaper, or more fundamental than the two reference assumptions they replace. In particular, the soft route assumes the asymptotic particle/S-matrix structure, the bootstrap assumes its free gauge field and local completion rule, and the geometric route assumes much of the metric language whose emergence a deeper theory might be asked to explain. No net ToE assumption compression is claimed.

## Premise and loophole stress test

| Question | Bounded disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Exact Lorentz invariance, locality, and a conventional S-matrix? | Weinberg's 1964 result explicitly uses Lorentz invariance and pole structure. A candidate without the relevant asymptotic S-matrix, or without the pole/factorization premise, is outside this proof; it has not thereby defeated the observed low-energy Lorentz/GR constraints. Locality is an explicit premise of the Deser construction, not a substitute for checking Weinberg's stated premises. |
| One massless spin-2 field, or extra scalars/spin-2 states? | A scalar can mediate an additional composition-dependent force while the leading massless-spin-2 soft coupling remains universal. Multiple massless spin-2 fields change the starting class; they are **not** an unrestricted escape, since [Boulanger *et al.* (2001)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0007220) rule out cross-interactions under their finite-field, ghost-free, at-most-two-derivative premises. |
| Does on-shell gauge decoupling equal full diffeomorphism invariance? | No. The 1964 soft result is an on-shell condition; the local bootstrap constructs a nonlinear diffeomorphism-invariant completion under extra premises. Lovelock assumes a metric-covariant setting. These claims must not be merged. |
| Curved or non-asymptotically-flat backgrounds? | The flat S-matrix argument is not a general cosmological theorem. Deser's later discussion allows a constant-curvature-background bootstrap with a cosmological term, but that is another specified setup rather than a value predicted for $\rho_\Lambda$. [Deser 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2975). |
| Unique equation, or field-redefinition class? | The bootstrap is a representative local completion; equivalent field choices can relocate interactions. Lovelock constrains a metric-equation class, not every matter coupling or coefficient. Compare invariant observables rather than action labels. |
| Higher-curvature and nonminimal operators? | They are outside the two-derivative uniqueness claim and remain in the joint EFT ledger. In particular, a choice of minimal matter coupling cannot delete $\xi RH^\dagger H$ from the renormalized theory. |

## Cheapest candidate-specific attacks and admission decision

For a **future specified candidate**, the cheapest checks would be: (1) derive its soft pole and gauge/Ward identity and look for species-dependent $\kappa_i$ under the theorem's premises; (2) test whether its cubic spin-2/matter vertices preserve the free gauge constraints and whether the completion is equivalent to Einstein-Hilbert at the declared two-derivative order; (3) state exactly which Lovelock premises hold and which extra fields/derivatives evade them. Each check must keep boundary, field-redefinition, $\xi$, and higher-order EFT effects visible. Failure of a premise limits the theorem's applicability; it does not alone falsify the candidate. [Weinberg 1964](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049); [Deser 1970](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411023); [Lovelock 1971](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1665613).

**NONE_FOR_ACTIVE_SEAM.** There is no frozen ToE-Next source construction whose independent assumptions or amplitudes can now be tested against one of these derivations. Recomputing the established theorem for its own sake would not decide whether the project's low-energy ledger has achieved net compression. Reopen only when a specified candidate provides a massless spin-2 sector, a declared background and matter set, a leading coupling or two-derivative completion to test, and an observable/authority ceiling. No gravity calculation, UV-framework selection, or repository machinery is authorized by this audit.

## Primary-source use and limits

- [Weinberg (1964)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049): soft S-matrix universality under the paper's stated pole/Lorentz/massless-spin premises; not a curved-space EFT basis.
- [Weinberg (1965)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.138.B988): perturbative S-matrix connection to Einstein equations under additional physical-helicity/long-range-interaction assumptions; not interchangeable with the narrower 1964 result.
- [Deser (1970)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411023) and [Deser (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2975): local first-order self-coupling construction and clarification, not an assumption-free or all-derivative uniqueness theorem.
- [Lovelock (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1665613): four-dimensional metric-tensor uniqueness within stated local second-order premises, not a derivation of metric ontology or universal matter coupling.
- [Callan, Coleman & Jackiw (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(70)90394-5) and [Markkanen *et al.* (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02020): stress-tensor improvement and curved-SM $\xi$ renormalization limit any claim that minimal coupling is uniquely fixed.
- [Boulanger, Damour, Gualtieri & Henneaux (2001)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0007220): bounded no-cross-coupling result for finite collections of free-limit Pauli-Fierz fields with at most two derivatives; not a prohibition of all multi-field gravity models.
