# Quantum-to-quasiclassical recovery reference audit

**Disposition:** conditional recovery mechanisms and a reference ledger established; **active seam: NONE**; **calculation started: NO**. This audits what standard quantum theory already explains in specified regimes. It does not select an interpretation, claim that decoherence alone solves measurement, or replace the separate [scientific baseline](SCIENTIFIC_BASELINE.md) obligation.

## Question and authority ceiling

What does a candidate that modifies quantum or gravitational foundations have to recover between quantum predictions and observed classical behavior? The target is **not** a universal quantum-to-classical length or a new physical postulate. It is a model-dependent chain: suppression of relevant interference, selection and persistence of useful coarse-grained variables, probability rules for those variables, and approximate classical evolution over a declared domain and time. These are distinct claims. The existence of a definite experienced outcome and the status of the Born rule are additional foundational questions, not automatic consequences of suppressing reduced-state off-diagonal terms. [Zurek (2003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105127); [Schlosshauer (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312059).

The audit assumes standard quantum dynamics as the *reference* to be recovered. It does not infer quantum cosmology or classical spacetime from the open-system examples. A future gravity or quantum-foundations candidate must supply its own state, observables, system/environment or coarse-graining construction, and quantitative limits before this ledger can judge it.

## Mechanism comparison

| Mechanism | Controlled result in its stated setting | Required inputs and limit | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Environment-induced decoherence** | Entanglement with an environment can suppress interference between selected alternatives in the *reduced* state, making local interference inaccessible to a stated accuracy and time scale. [Joos & Zeh (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01725541); [Zurek (2003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105127). | Interaction Hamiltonian, system/environment split, initial correlations or state, monitored observable, decoherence rate, and recurrence/validity interval. | The total state need not collapse. An approximately diagonal reduced density matrix is not automatically one realized outcome or an ignorance mixture. |
| **Einselection / pointer stability** | In specified system-bath models, interaction dynamics favors states that retain predictive stability; for a damped oscillator, coherent states emerge under a predictability criterion. [Zurek, Habib & Paz (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.1187). | A coupling and stability criterion, plus the domain in which the preferred set is robust. | A universal pointer basis, classical trajectories for every system, or outcome selection. |
| **Decoherent or consistent histories** | For a declared coarse-grained family with negligible off-diagonal decoherence functional, its diagonal weights obey approximate classical probability sum rules. Certain families can also concentrate on histories obeying effective classical equations with noise. [Halliwell (1994)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9407040); [Gell-Mann & Hartle (1993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210010). | Initial density operator, class operators/coarse graining, approximate consistency criterion, stability and predictability tests. | Consistency alone does not uniquely select a quasiclassical family; different consistent sets can support different inferences. It does not by itself choose a single actual history. [Dowker & Kent (1995)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9409037). |
| **Wigner/Moyal and Ehrenfest/semiclassical limits** | Quantum phase-space evolution can approach classical Liouville evolution, and expectation values can track classical equations, in controlled smooth-potential, narrow-state, scale, and time regimes. [Moyal (1949)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100000487). | A specified state, observable, Hamiltonian, small correction parameter or error estimate, and validity time. In particular, $d\langle p\rangle/dt=-\langle\nabla V\rangle$ equals $-\nabla V(\langle x\rangle)$ only under additional conditions (exactly for quadratic $V$). | A Wigner function is generally a quasiprobability, not a positive classical distribution. A classical-looking mean trajectory is not a definite individual path or long-time classicality. [Hudson (1974)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0034-4877(74)90007-X). |
| **Quantum Brownian motion / open-system stochastic limits** | Defined bath models yield decoherence, dissipation and noise; the Caldeira-Leggett influence-functional construction reaches a classical Fokker-Planck limit, while the Hu-Paz-Zhang linear-coupling model treats more general spectral environments, including memory and colored noise. [Caldeira & Leggett (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4371(83)90013-4); [Hu, Paz & Zhang (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.45.2843). | Bath spectrum, coupling, temperature/state, initial correlations, approximation and time scales. Markovian/white-noise assumptions require justification if invoked. | Universal deterministic Newtonian dynamics, an interpretation-independent single outcome, or a general quantum-gravity limit. |
| **Quantum Darwinism / environmental records** | In models where pointer information is redundantly imprinted in separate environmental fragments, independent observers can access concordant records without directly disturbing the system. This strengthens an *operational* notion of objectivity. [Ollivier, Poulin & Zurek (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408125). | A specific environment partition, accessible fragments, information measure, and demonstrated redundancy. | Redundancy for all physical systems, an ontological unique event, or Born probabilities from record proliferation alone. |

These mechanisms are complementary, not six competing ontologies. A semiclassical expansion can describe motion while failing to suppress interference; a reduced state can decohere rapidly while its preferred variables remain dynamically unstable; redundant records can stabilize operational agreement without selecting a fundamental interpretation.

## Recovery ledger

| Recovery obligation | Status of established quantum theory | Remaining assumptions / admission test for a deeper candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suppression of observable interference | **Conditional quantitative recovery** in many specified open-system models. | Identify alternatives, coupling, state, suppression measure, time scale, and possible recoherence. Reduced-state suppression is not destruction of global coherence. |
| Stable pointer or coarse-grained variables | **Conditional recovery** under selected interactions and predictability criteria. | Specify the split and observables; demonstrate stability over the observational interval rather than presuming position or a preferred basis. |
| Approximately classical dynamics | **Conditional recovery** through semiclassical and open-system limits; effective motion may include fluctuations, dissipation and noise. | Show equations and error/time bounds for the proposed variables. Ehrenfest means alone do not guarantee trajectories; decoherence alone does not guarantee predictable dynamics. |
| Classical probability *calculus* for alternatives | **Conditional recovery within a decoherent family:** interference between its histories is negligible, so diagonal quantum weights approximately satisfy additive sum rules. | Declare the family, consistency tolerance and coarse graining. The numerical weights use the quantum state and Born/trace rule; alternative incompatible families cannot be combined indiscriminately. |
| Shared, robust macroscopic records | **Conditional operational recovery** when pointer information is stable and demonstrably redundant. | Identify record carriers, redundancy and observational access; distinguish this from a metaphysical claim that one outcome was selected. |
| One definite outcome | **Not derived by unitary decoherence alone.** | A candidate claiming more must state its additional ontology, dynamics or interpretive postulates and any distinct observable consequence. Lack of such a derivation is not evidence for a particular interpretation. |
| Born-rule weights | **Input to the standard probability assignment; not a consequence of interference suppression alone.** | Separate derivations that add premises (such as envariance, decision theory or other interpretive structure) from decoherence itself; state exactly which assumptions and empirical consequences are new. [Schlosshauer & Fine (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312058). |

For histories $\alpha,\beta$ with class operators $C_\alpha$, the diagnostic is

$$
D(\alpha,\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}(C_\alpha\rho C_\beta^\dagger).
$$

If the relevant off-diagonal terms are negligible at a declared tolerance, $p(\alpha)=D(\alpha,\alpha)$ approximately obeys the sum rules **for that family**. This is a consistency condition on probability assignments, not a proof that exactly one $\alpha$ occurs. Quasiclassicality further requires histories concentrated around stable effective equations; decoherence and predictability can demand different amounts of coarse graining. [Halliwell (1994)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9407040); [Gell-Mann & Hartle (1993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210010).

## Assumptions and future use

The reference mechanisms carry independent inputs: quantum dynamics and state; system/environment factorization where used; interaction and bath spectrum; initial correlations; coarse-grained observables and tolerances; semiclassical scale and time; and the Born/trace probability rule. A candidate that merely relabels these inputs has not compressed assumptions. A candidate that modifies quantum theory must recover the tested operational predictions and the appropriate entries above, while making its extra outcome or probability claims separately accountable. A candidate that modifies gravity must additionally exhibit a controlled quasiclassical spacetime and matter regime rather than extrapolating a laboratory bath model to the universe.

**NONE_FOR_ACTIVE_SEAM.** No presently frozen ToE-Next source theory specifies a quantum-to-classical map, state, coarse graining, observable, error tolerance and discriminator whose calculation could resolve a missing arrow. The baseline requirement remains active as a *general recovery obligation*, but there is no active seam or calculation. Reopen this audit only for such a specified candidate or a sharply defined empirical failure of the established reference mechanisms. No new interpretation, tool, framework, or repository architecture is selected here.

## Primary-source boundaries

- [Joos & Zeh (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01725541) and [Zurek (2003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0105127): environmental decoherence and einselection, not a general outcome theorem.
- [Zurek, Habib & Paz (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.1187): oscillator pointer-state example, not a universal preferred basis.
- [Gell-Mann & Hartle (1993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9210010), [Halliwell (1994)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9407040), and [Dowker & Kent (1995)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9409037): conditional quasiclassical histories, probability sum rules and set-selection limitation.
- [Moyal (1949)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100000487) and [Hudson (1974)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0034-4877(74)90007-X): phase-space correspondence and a pure-state positivity restriction, not an unrestricted long-time classical limit.
- [Caldeira & Leggett (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4371(83)90013-4) and [Hu, Paz & Zhang (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.45.2843): bath-specific stochastic and decoherence mechanisms.
- [Ollivier, Poulin & Zurek (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408125): redundant-record criterion for operational objectivity, not unique-outcome proof.
- [Schlosshauer (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312059) and [Schlosshauer & Fine (2005)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312058): interpretive and Born-derivation limits; not an endorsement of one interpretation.
